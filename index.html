IN A NUTSHELL
This nutshell plot brings together the two central findings of this study into a single view. Each point represents one of the 17 Philippine regions, plotted against its 2023 poverty incidence. The vertical axis tracks how synchronized a region's food price spikes were with other regions (RQ1), while the lower axis tracks how volatile its food prices were overall (RQ2). Green points indicate low to mid poverty regions, while pink points indicate high poverty regions.

Two clear downward trends emerge from the chart. The blue dashed line shows that regions with lower poverty tend to have higher synchronization scores, meaning their food price spikes move more in step with national patterns.

The pink dashed line shows that these same lower poverty regions also experience greater price volatility. Both regression lines carry strong negative correlations, with r values of -0.69 and -0.73 respectively, confirming that poverty incidence is meaningfully linked to both measures.

Regions like NCR, Region I, and Region II cluster in the upper left, showing high sync scores and relatively low poverty. In contrast, high poverty regions such as BARMM, Region IX, and Region VIII sit toward the lower right, with weaker synchronization and lower volatility. This pattern holds consistently across the dataset, suggesting it is not a coincidence but a structural difference in how regions connect to national food markets.

Taken together, the chart supports the paper's core argument: lower poverty regions are more integrated into national price networks, making them more exposed to widespread price shocks. Higher poverty regions, on the other hand, are more economically isolated, which insulates them from national price movements but also leaves them more vulnerable to local supply disruptions that are harder to anticipate and address.
