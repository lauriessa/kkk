VISUALIZATION
To help visualize and better understand the relationship between the variables, a scatter plot was used to display the association between poverty incidence and food price volatility across regions. Each point in the graph represents a region, positioned according to its corresponding poverty level (x-axis) and volatility measure (y-axis).

The scatter plot ABOVE shows a downward-sloping trendline, indicating a negative relationship between the two variables. The data points follow a relatively consistent downward pattern, implying that the relationship is not random. While some variation exists across regions, the overall distribution supports the presence of an inverse association between poverty and volatility. Furthermore, the pattern appears approximately linear, as the points cluster around the fitted line without significant curvature or directional shifts.
HYPOTHESIS TESTING
As the data consists of cross-sectional regional observations, we analyzed the relationship between food price volatility and poverty incidence using both Pearson and Spearman correlation tests. These tests are appropriate for determining whether a statistically significant association exists between two variables.

The Pearson correlation test measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship, while the Spearman correlation test assesses a monotonic relationship, which does not require linearity and is more robust to non-normal data and outliers. Using both methods ensures that the results are consistent and reliable.

This approach aligns with the objective of the study: to determine whether variations in food price volatility are associated with differences in poverty levels across regions.

For both tests, the hypotheses are defined as follows:

  • Null Hypothesis: There is no relationship between food price volatility and poverty incidence.
  • Alternative Hypothesis: There is a relationship between food price volatility and poverty incidence.

A significance level of alpha = 0.05 was used. When the p-value is less than 0.05, the null hypothesis is rejected, indicating a statistically significant relationship.

Since both p-values are less than the significance level, we reject the null hypothesis in both cases. This indicates, with 95% confidence, that there is a statistically significant relationship between food price volatility and poverty incidence.

Additionally, both correlation coefficients are negative (r = -0.7320; rho = -0.7304), indicating that the relationship is inverse in direction.
DISCUSSION
The scatter plot, together with the Pearson and Spearman correlation tests, indicates that there is a significant inverse relationship between regional price volatility from 2021 to 2025 and the region’s poverty incidence among the population. The graph showed a downward-sloping trendline, suggesting that regions with higher poverty incidence generally experienced lower food price volatility, while regions with lower poverty incidence tended to experience greater price fluctuations. The distribution of the data points also suggests that the relationship between the two variables is relatively consistent across regions.

This visual observation was supported by the statistical results of the correlation tests. The Pearson correlation coefficient of −0.7320 indicates a strong negative linear relationship between regional price volatility and poverty incidence. Similarly, the Spearman correlation coefficient of −0.7304 also reflects a strong negative association, confirming that the relationship remains consistent even when analyzed using ranked data.

The p-values obtained from both tests P=0.0008 and p=0.0009 are lower than the significance level of 0.05 which means that the relationship is statistically significant. Therefore, the null hypothesis stating that there is no significant relationship between regional price volatility and poverty incidence is rejected.

The similarity between the graphical trend and the statistical test results strengthens the reliability of the findings. Both the visualization and the correlation analyses consistently show that higher poverty incidence is associated with lower levels of food price volatility.
