CONCLUSION
This study looked at how food price spikes affect different regions in the Philippines from 2021 to 2025. It tracked seven basic food items: rice, meat, beef, cabbage, eggplants, eggs, and tomatoes.

The research found that price spikes do not happen at the same time in all regions. Low-poverty regions like NCR, Region I, Region III, and CAR tend to move together, meaning they experience price changes at similar times. High-poverty regions, especially in Visayas and Mindanao, show more irregular and unpredictable price behavior. The Mann-Whitney U test confirmed that this difference is statistically significant (p = 0.0152).

The study also found a significant inverse relationship between food price volatility and poverty incidence. Both the Pearson (r = -0.7320) and Spearman (rho = -0.7304) tests confirmed this with very low p-values (0.0008 and 0.0009). This means that regions with higher poverty levels actually show lower price synchronization, not because they are better off, but because their food prices are more locally driven and harder to predict. These areas are more vulnerable to sudden and isolated price shocks.

In short, where you live in the Philippines determines how much food price volatility you experience, and poor regions bear the most unpredictable burden.
RECOMMENDATION
For policymakers and government agencies
$1
Targeted food price monitoring systems should be set up in high-poverty regions, especially in Visayas and Mindanao. Since these areas experience irregular, locally driven price behavior, a one-size-fits-all national pricing policy may not be sufficient. Region-specific interventions are needed.
For food supply chain planners
$2
Improving transportation infrastructure and logistics in remote and high-poverty areas can help reduce the gap between national food price trends and local prices. Better roads and lower transport costs would help stabilize food prices in these regions.
For social protection programs
$3
Cash aid or food subsidies should be more responsive in high-poverty areas. Since price spikes in these regions are harder to predict and do not follow national patterns, assistance programs need to be flexible and able to respond quickly to local price changes.
For future researcH
$4
This study focused on correlation and synchronization. Future studies should examine the specific causes of price irregularities in high-poverty regions, including the roles of local supply chains, climate events, and post-harvest losses. A longer time range and more commodities could also strengthen the findings.
